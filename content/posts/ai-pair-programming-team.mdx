---
title: What AI pair programming actually changed on my team
date: 2026-05-22
tag: ai
excerpt: Not velocity. Review depth.
readMins: 6
---

The promise of AI pair programming was always speed — ship more, faster. Six months in, that's not the change I'd point to. What actually shifted is how much context shows up in a pull request: descriptions that explain the "why," tests written alongside the change instead of bolted on after a review comment, edge cases called out before anyone asks.

Part of that is because writing the explanation is now cheap enough that people do it. Part of it is that a tool willing to ask "what happens if this list is empty" at 11pm catches things a reviewer would otherwise catch at 9am the next day, a full review cycle later.

The velocity gain, when it shows up, is a side effect of fewer round trips — not fewer people thinking. That's the distinction I keep making to folks who ask if this replaces engineers on the team. It replaces the boring first pass. The judgment calls are still very much ours.
